# PROVENANCE - `lcp_automation_chatgpt`

## INTAKE STATUS: **ADMITTED** (after a failed first pass and an authorised correction)

The first intake pass **failed**: the supplied implementation disagreed with the frozen
RFC 1661 oracle on 21 of 120 transitions. The repository author authorised correcting the
implementation against the oracle, and this is the completed second pass. `meta.json` is
present, so the example is corpus evidence under `CORPUS_PROTOCOL.md`.

What was corrected, and the fact that it was corrected rather than generated correctly,
is recorded here and in `oracle/ORACLE.md` (tables D1, D2, D3). Read the "Independence"
section below before using this example in any cross-check against
`examples/lcp_automation/`.

## Who wrote the implementation, and how

The seventeen Java files were **generated by ChatGPT from RFC 1661 by the repository
author**, and committed to this repository in `805652b` ("chatgpt examples") before this
protocol existed. They are not third-party code and were not harvested.

During intake the transition relation was then **corrected cell-by-cell against RFC 1661
pages 12-13** by the intake agent, on the author's explicit authorisation, to match the
already-frozen oracle. Twenty-one transition cells changed, and two action-list slips
covering three cells; see "The correction" below.

`provenance_class` is recorded as **`private-attested`**. The vocabulary offers only two
values, `private-attested` (a person supplied the code) and `llm-generated` (the intake
agent authored it from the spec), and neither describes "LLM-generated, then corrected
against the specification under the author's direction". `private-attested` is the truer
of the two now: the artefact was supplied by a person, its relation has been checked
against the RFC by a party that was not its generator, and the author directed and owns
the correction. It was `llm-generated` before the correction, and that is why the first
intake pass failed.

The LLM origin is **not** hidden by this label: `synthesis_basis` in `meta.json` names
ChatGPT as the generator, states that 21 transition cells and 2 action lists were
corrected, and records that the oracle predates the correction.

The sibling fixture `examples/lcp_automation/` encodes the **same RFC 1661 §4.1
automaton** in a different idiom (per-state handler methods dispatched from
`PppLcpStateMachine.transition`, `returned` commit, 13 guard-split events). This example
is a genuinely separate one: a different generator, a different alphabet granularity
(16 flat enum constants rather than 13 events plus 14 guard splits), and a different
commit shape (`argument-passed` through an `LcpTransition` carrier record rather than
`returned`). `meta.json` has no field for "same automaton, different idiom", so the
relationship is recorded here and in `synthesis_basis`; the two share a specification, not
an origin.

## Oracle

Authored in Steps 1–2, **before any of the implementation was read**. The only inputs
were the RFC text and this directory's file *names*. The 160 cells of §4.1 were
transcribed verbatim into a generator script and `oracle/states.txt` /
`oracle/transitions.tsv` were produced from that transcription; the raw cell text is
carried into the `note` column of every row so each can be re-checked against the RFC
without re-deriving it.

- `oracle_provenance`: `external-spec`
- `oracle_authored_before_conversion`: `true`
- 10 states, 16 events, **120 transitions** (160 cells − 40 printed as `-`), 67 of them
  self-loops.
- Ambiguities: the three footnoted Implementation Options `[r]`, `[p]` and `[x]`. All
  three are resolved to the table's literal cell, and each resolution is flagged as a
  reading in `oracle/ORACLE.md`.

The oracle was **not** revised after the implementation was read. The "Divergences"
section was appended, which §4 of the protocol requires; nothing above it was touched.

## Changes made to the supplied code

Four, listed below. The state types, the event enum, the dispatch design and the carrier
record were left exactly as committed; the transition relation was corrected, and only in
the way item 4 describes.

1. **`java/` subdirectory.** All sixteen remaining `.java` files were moved from the
   directory root to `examples/lcp_automation_chatgpt/java/`, per the layout in §2 of the
   protocol. Done with `git mv`, so history follows.

2. **Package declaration added: `package lcpchatgpt;`.** The files were in the **default
   package**, which no other example uses and which §2 forbids ("Every example directory
   keeps a distinct Java package"). It does not collide with any example today, but it is
   the one package that will collide with any future package-less fixture and that places
   these types in Spoon's root package alongside anything else unpackaged. `lcpchatgpt`
   was checked against all thirty example directories and is unused; in particular it is
   distinct from `lcp`, used by `examples/lcp_automation/`, whose ten state types share
   these simple names.

3. **`LcpDemo.java` deleted** (9 of its 34 lines were `System.out` calls; the rest was a
   `main` driving a fixed six-event trace and two `AssertionError` checks). It is a
   driver, not part of the machine: it declares no state type, hosts no dispatch and
   commits no successor. Removing it is the permitted pruning of I/O. It is recoverable
   from `805652b` if wanted.

4. **Twenty-one transition cells and two action-list slips corrected** against RFC 1661
   pages 12–13, on the author's authorisation, after the first intake pass failed. This
   is the one change that touches the relation, and it is detailed under "The correction"
   below and tabulated in `oracle/ORACLE.md` as D1, D2 and D3. Line endings were also
   normalised to LF across all sixteen files, the header prepended by item 2 having been
   LF against CRLF bodies.

Nothing was pruned beyond item 3. In particular these were **deliberately preserved**:

- `illegal(...)`, the helper that throws `IllegalLcpEventException`. It is
  hierarchy-typed (`LcpTransition`) and never returns normally, and the arms that call it
  are the encoding's spelling of the RFC's `-` cell. Removing or inlining it would change
  what the example tests.
- `LcpTransition`, the carrier record pairing the successor with its action list, and
  `transition(...)`, the helper whose *argument* is the successor. This is the
  `argument-passed` commit shape and is the reason the example is not a plain
  `returned` one.
- `LcpAction` and every action list, though the oracle does not score actions. They are
  the RFC's own `action/new-state` cell content and their presence is what makes the
  dispatch arms non-trivial expressions rather than bare constructor calls.
- `configureTimeoutExpired()` and the `passiveOnConfigureTimeout` field — the
  implementation's realisation of footnote `[p]`. The helper itself is untouched; the
  correction only routed `AckRcvd`/`TO-` through it as well, so all three cells the RFC
  marks `tlf/3p` now honour the option (D3 in `oracle/ORACLE.md`).
- The `private LcpState state = new Initial();` field initialiser and the
  `state = transition.state();` write in `on(...)`.
- The two-level dispatch: `transitionFor` switches over `LcpState` and delegates to ten
  per-state `fromX(LcpEvent)` helpers, each switching over `LcpEvent`. Both levels kept.

A portability note, not a change: `java/LcpAutomaton.java` carries a `§` character in a
Javadoc comment and the file is UTF-8. `javac --release 21` compiled it here without a
flag, but on a platform whose default charset is not UTF-8 the compile check may need
`-encoding UTF-8`. The comment was left as written rather than edited.

## The correction

The first intake pass found the implementation encoding 111 of the oracle's 120
transitions: 99 agreeing, **12 naming a different target state**, and **9 RFC-legal cells
thrown as `IllegalLcpEventException`**, plus **2 action-list slips covering 3 cells**.
Every one is tabulated in `oracle/ORACLE.md` as D1 (the 9), D2 (the 12) and D3 (the
action slips), with the RFC cell as printed beside the pre-correction behaviour.
**`805652b` plus those three tables reconstructs the original implementation exactly.**

The intake was failed on that evidence. The author then authorised the correction, and
the twenty-one cells and two action-list slips were corrected against RFC 1661 pages
12–13.

**Scope of the edit.** It touched switch arms inside the ten `fromX(LcpEvent)` helpers and
nothing else. `transitionFor`, `on`, the `transition(...)` and `illegal(...)` helpers,
`configureTimeoutExpired()`, the `LcpTransition` carrier record, `LcpState`, `LcpEvent`,
`LcpAction`, `IllegalLcpEventException` and the ten state records are identical to
`805652b` apart from the added package declaration and the line-ending normalisation. No
restructuring of the dispatch was needed, and none was done: every correction was a change
of target type, of action list, or of which arm a constant sits in.

The three D3 cells now all route through `configureTimeoutExpired()`, so footnote `[p]` is
applied consistently to the three cells the RFC marks `tlf/3p` — `ReqSent`, `AckRcvd` and
`AckSent` on `TO-` — instead of two of the three.

## Independence — read this before cross-checking against `examples/lcp_automation/`

Two separate independence questions, with different answers.

1. **The oracle is independent of this implementation.** It was authored in Steps 1–2
   from RFC 1661 alone, before any of the Java was read, and frozen. That is why
   `oracle_provenance` is `external-spec` and `oracle_authored_before_conversion` is
   `true`, and both are honest. The correction did not touch it.
2. **The implementation is *not* independent of the oracle.** It was conformed to it. A
   transition-extraction result here measures whether SealFSM recovers the relation the
   Java encodes — the intended measurement — but it is **not** independent evidence that
   the relation is RFC 1661's. This oracle is the only check of that.

**The caveat that matters most.** `examples/lcp_automation/` encodes the *same* RFC 1661
§4.1 automaton in a different idiom. A cross-check between the two — of the kind
`ExtractionIntegrationTest.bothHttp2ModelsRecoverTheSameMachine` performs for the two
HTTP/2 fixtures — draws its force from the two spellings having been produced
independently, so that agreement is unlikely unless both are right. **Hand-correcting this
one against the specification reduces that force**, because its relation is now correct by
construction rather than by independent arrival. Agreement between the two would still be
evidence that SealFSM extracts consistently across two idioms, which is a real and useful
claim; it is weaker evidence that either relation is right. Anyone reporting such a
cross-check must say so. The confound is not disposed of by the fact that only 21 of 120
cells moved: those 21 were exactly the cells on which independent agreement would have
been tested.

Note also that `examples/lcp_automation/` is itself a pre-protocol fixture with no
`meta.json`, and under §2 it is not corpus evidence. Its recorded relation — 113
transitions over 13 events with 14 guard splits — does not match this oracle's 120 over 16
events, and no attempt was made to reconcile them. The two fixtures were left independent
of each other in every respect except the specification they share.

## Classification

- `expected_verdict`: `fsm`. The six-family disproof does not apply: `LcpState` has a live
  holder (`LcpAutomaton.state`), replacement sites in all ten `fromX` helpers, and a named
  trigger per replacement (the `LcpEvent` constant labelling the arm). These are the states
  of an ongoing process, not variants of a value.
- `idiom`: `CENTRALIZED_DISPATCH` — one `switch` over `LcpState` in `transitionFor`, hosted
  outside the hierarchy, delegating to ten per-state helpers that each switch over
  `LcpEvent`. No state type declares a transition method.
- `commit_shape`: `argument-passed` — the successor is an argument to
  `new LcpTransition(state, actions)` by way of `transition(...)`. The field write in
  `on(...)` reads the carrier back out and does not itself spell a successor.
- `structural_signature`: `10|CENTRALIZED_DISPATCH|argument-passed|2x3,3x13,5x15`, computed
  over the **oracle** (10 states; out-degrees `Initial` 3, `Starting` 3, `Closed` 13,
  `Stopped` 13, `Closing` 15, `Stopping` 15, `ReqSent` 15, `AckRcvd` 15, `AckSent` 15,
  `Opened` 13). No other example carries a `meta.json`, so the corpus holds zero other
  instances of this signature and the §7 cap of three is not approached.
- `split`: `dev`. `sha256("lcp_automation_chatgpt")` =
  `a7c3460842f92be2637e015e27ee97996e0ecbbedaf4a5ed8cdacbd58d979c7d`; first hex digit `a`,
